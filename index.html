Sources you can open

Grocery prices · BLS Consumer Price Index

Annual means of the monthly, not-seasonally-adjusted CPI-U “Food at home” series. National series: CUUR0000SAF11. Regional series: Northeast, Midwest, South, West. CPI is a price index, not dollars spent.

Income · BEA National Income and Product Accounts

Personal income per capita, annual dollars, not seasonally adjusted. “Personal income” includes multiple income sources; it is not household wages, take-home pay, or median income. The chart compares each year’s nominal income growth with that year’s food-price change.

Food budget share · BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey

Table 1800, Census regions and Table 2700, Census divisions. BLS classifies food by where it is purchased: food-store purchases are “at home,” while restaurant takeout and delivery are “away from home,” even if eaten at home. Values are average annual dollars for the selected two-year period; the map computes food-at-home mean ÷ income-before-taxes mean. The player includes five shared periods, 2017–2018 through 2021–2022. BLS lists newer spreadsheets for 2022–2023 and 2023–2024 at its geographic table index; those are not included in this release. Census regions and divisions. State outlines come from the us-atlas TopoJSON project, based on U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundaries. Outlines help orient; the statistics belong to the grouped Census geography.

How to read the two measures
  • Annual price change: the chart compares each year’s change in annual-average national food-at-home CPI-U and BEA personal income per capita. The map shows regional CPI-U percent change from the prior calendar year, 1979–2024. Division outlines inherit the containing region’s rate because this package has no comparable division CPI series.
  • Food budget share: BLS mean annual food-at-home expenditure ÷ BLS mean annual income before taxes × 100. The player shows five overlapping two-calendar-year means from 2017–2018 through 2021–2022; these are not single-year estimates. BLS lists newer spreadsheets for 2022–2023 and 2023–2024, which are not included here. The ratio of means does not equal the mean of individual consumer-unit ratios.
  • Not a full affordability measure: CPI and personal income per capita describe broad trends, not a grocery basket for a particular person. Neither line accounts for taxes, benefits, household size, housing costs, income distribution, or changes in shopping behavior.
  • Map: colors group state outlines into the selected Census division/region. Select a division from the map or the synchronized list. A state does not have its own observation in this graphic.
  • Data vintage: annual CPI and income series are packaged through 2024. BLS regional/division spending rows are observed geographic mean tables for 2017–2018 through 2021–2022. Snapshot dates differ by source.
